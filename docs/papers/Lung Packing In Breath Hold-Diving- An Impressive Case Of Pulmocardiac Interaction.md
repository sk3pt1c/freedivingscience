## Why This Matters for Freedivers
This case report shows, with cardiac MRI, that lung packing can significantly reduce stroke volume and cardiac output even in a healthy, elite diver. That’s the “mechanical” reason packing can cause dizziness or fainting and why aggressive packing on land should be treated as a real safety risk, not just a performance trick.

## Synopsis
Lung packing (glossopharyngeal insufflation) is used by many advanced freedivers to push lung volume above normal full inhale. The goal is practical: more air for equalisation at depth and a bit more oxygen stored in the lungs. But the trade-off is that stuffing the chest with extra air increases pressure inside the thorax and can physically interfere with circulation.

This paper is a simple but powerful illustration of that trade-off. The authors report on a healthy 40-year-old competitive breath-hold diver who performed lung packing followed by a dry apnea, while the team measured heart function using cardiac MRI and aortic flow measurements. Cardiac MRI is helpful here because it can show both the shape/volume of the heart chambers and how much blood is actually moving through the aorta.

The headline result is big: after lung packing and during the apnea, the diver’s stroke volume dropped from 92 ml (normal breathing) to 48 ml. Cardiac output also fell markedly (about 4.6 L/min down to 3.2 L/min). On the images, both right and left ventricular volumes were smaller by the end of the apnea, which the authors interpret as a combination of reduced venous return (less blood getting back to the heart) and increased intrapulmonary/intrathoracic pressure. In other words, the heart has less room to fill, so it pumps less blood each beat.

For freedivers, the key message is not “never pack,” but “packing has a real cardiovascular cost.” Even when everything is done deliberately in a controlled setting, the technique can meaningfully reduce the amount of blood the heart pumps. That helps explain why some divers feel light-headed after packing and why minimal, conservative packing (and careful posture/monitoring) matters if the technique is used at all.

## Abstract
There is a complex interaction between the heart and the lungs. We report on a healthy female who performs breath hold diving at a high, international level. In order to optimize pressure equalization during diving and to increase oxygen available, apneists employed a special breathing maneuver, so called “lung packing”. Based on cardiac MRI we could demonstrate impressive effects of this maneuver on left ventricular geometry and hemodynamics. Beyond the fact, that our findings support the concept of pulmonary–cardiac interrelationship, it should be emphasized, that the reported, extreme breathing maneuver could have detrimental consequences due to reduction of stroke volume and cardiac output.
